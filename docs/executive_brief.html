Where the semiconductor supply network actually breaks

Network model of 2,690 firms and 14,932 supply agreements · $1.3T of modelled annual production

Bottom line. The network survives any single supplier failure — the worst costs 3.4% of production. But the suppliers that matter are not the ones spend analysis finds, and one input line in five has no qualified second source behind it.

What we found

$43.6Bworst single failure

Three mid-chain suppliers each halt more than $36B of production. A power-module assembler in the Philippines, a sensor-module plant in Xi'an, and a packaging house in Xi'an. None of the three is a large supplier by spend. Twenty-six suppliers in total halt more than 1% of output.

21%of input lines

1,297 of 6,178 bill-of-material lines have exactly one qualified source. Lithography equipment has no second source two-thirds of the time. Requalifying a tool vendor or a photoresist takes 12–24 months, so these are not positions that can be recovered inside a fiscal year.

580days worst lead time

Fabrication plants are the most exposed buyers in the network — typically six of their nine inputs sole-sourced, behind lead times above 500 days. They are simultaneously the least damaging tier to lose, at 0.3%, because their customers hold alternatives. Exposure and criticality run in opposite directions.

44 / 45ports replaceable

Logistics is not the exposure it appears to be. Every modelled seaport and air hub but one causes zero production loss when closed, because freight reroutes through an alternative gateway within weeks. The exception is the sole gateway serving Australian raw materials.

84%from East Asia

Geographic concentration is the systemic risk, not any individual firm. Taiwan alone holds 44% of fabrication value; Taiwan and Korea together hold 69%. A regional event removes many suppliers at once, and the redundancy that protects against single failures does not protect against that.

Recommended actions

  1. Qualify second sources for the 26 suppliers above the 1% threshold. That list is small enough to action this year and is attached in full.
  2. Prioritise by replaceability, not by spend. The suppliers that appear repeatedly among the most damaging failures are packaging-substrate, bonding-wire and process-gas firms — low unit cost, low total spend, and no qualified alternative at the buyers that depend on them. Spend-based ranking does not surface any of them.
  3. Treat regional concentration separately from supplier redundancy. Dual-sourcing within Taiwan does not reduce Taiwan exposure.
  4. Do not spend on logistics redundancy beyond the single sole-gateway route identified.

How this was measured

Each of 1,870 suppliers was removed from the network in turn and the consequences traced forward. A plant is treated as stopped when any one of its required inputs loses every qualified supplier — not merely when it loses a supplier.

Failures then propagate: a stopped plant's own customers are re-assessed, and so on downstream.

What this does not capture

Capacity. Three qualified suppliers counts as covered here. In practice it is only covered if those three hold spare capacity to absorb the fourth's volume — which, in this industry, they rarely do.

This understates fabrication risk specifically, and is the first extension worth funding.
